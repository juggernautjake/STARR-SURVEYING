# 03 · Elevation certificate, and does not know what one is

**Who is calling** — Marisol Aguirre-Bennett, 1.5 acres near Nolanville, told by her insurer to get "an elevation thing".

**What this is testing** — The agent must not pretend to know what she needs, and must not talk her into or out of a product. It should capture the confusion accurately rather than tidying it into "elevation certificate".

**Roughly** 4 minutes · **awkward**

---

## The call

**1.** “Hi, um, my insurance company said I need to get an elevation — an elevation certificate? For my flood insurance. I don't really know what that is. My name is Marisol Aguirre-Bennett. I've got about an acre and a half out near Nolanville. Can somebody explain it to me and tell me what it costs?”

   > 👀 Hyphenated double surname said once, quickly. This should come back as uncertain and get spelled.

**2.** “Yeah okay.”

**3.** “It's A-G-U-I-R-R-E, hyphen, B-E-N-N-E-T-T. Marisol is M-A-R-I-S-O-L.”

   > 👀 After this the name should be settled and never raised again.

**4.** “254-555-0164.”

**5.** “So is that the same as a survey? Because the bank did a survey when we bought it. I don't understand why I need another one.”

   > 👀 It should NOT explain the difference as if it were the surveyor. A short honest "that is a good question for Hank" is the right answer.

**6.** “Okay. Well, I hope so, because they said my premium goes up if I don't have it.”

**7.** “It's 4419 Old Nolanville Road. There's a house and a detached garage.”

**8.** “Um, I have the paperwork from the insurance company. Does that help?”

**9.** “Marisol dot A dot Bennett at outlook dot com. Do you want me to spell it?”

**10.** “M-A-R-I-S-O-L, dot, A, dot, B-E-N-N-E-T-T, at outlook dot com.”

**11.** “No, I think that's it. Thank you for your help.”

---

## It should end up with

- firstName Marisol
- lastName Aguirre-Bennett spelled
- propertyAddress 4419 Old Nolanville Road
- acreage 1.5 acres
- structures house and detached garage
- surveyType elevation certificate (she is unsure)
- surveyPurpose flood insurance
- documents insurance paperwork

## Notes

The failure to watch for is the agent confidently explaining elevation certificates. It is a receptionist, not a surveyor, and a wrong explanation here costs a customer.
