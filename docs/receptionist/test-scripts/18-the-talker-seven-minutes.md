# 18 · The seven-minute caller

**Who is calling** — Lovely man. Will talk until the phone dies. Every answer becomes a story.

**What this is testing** — THE WIND-DOWN. This call must reach the seven-minute cap and the agent must end it warmly rather than stopping mid-sentence.

**Roughly** 7 minutes · **hard**

---

## The call

**1.** “Well hello there! I'm calling about a survey. Now, before I get into it, let me tell you why — my wife and I have been out here on this place since 1994, and back then there was nothing out here, I mean nothing, the road wasn't even paved...”

**2.** “Oh sure, sure, ask me anything.”

**3.** “Harold. Harold Wetherspoon. W-E-T-H-E-R-S-P-O-O-N. Like the pub, my son says, though I've never been.”

**4.** “254-555-0111. Had that number since — well, since we got the landline, so a long time.”

**5.** “The place is 9400 Old Cemetery Road, out toward Oglesby. Now the name of that road is a whole story in itself because there IS a cemetery, and my neighbour's people are in it...”

**6.** “Oh, about thirty-one acres. Thirty-one and change. It was forty when we bought it but we sold the back off to my brother-in-law, and THAT was an experience...”

**7.** “Well there's the house, the original house, and we put an addition on in '02. Then there's the shop, the hay barn, the old barn which is more of a suggestion at this point, two sheds, and the well house.”

**8.** “Why do I need it? My daughter's after us to get the estate sorted. She's a practical girl, takes after her mother. She sat us down at Thanksgiving—”

**9.** “Yes, I own it, me and my wife, joint. Forty-one years married in March.”

**10.** “Email? Oh, my wife has one. It's — Linda! What's the email? — she says it's lwetherspoon at — hold on — at gmail.”

**11.** “L-W-E-T-H-E-R-S-P-O-O-N. At gmail. Yes.”

**12.** “Documents, documents. There's the deed, there's a survey somewhere from when we bought, and there's the plat from when we split the back off. I'd have to dig.”

**13.** “Now, can I ask you something? When the fellow comes out, does he need me to walk it with him? Because I can, I know every corner of this place, I put half those fenceposts in myself—”

**14.** “And how long does it take him? Because I'll make sure I'm here. I'm always here, mind you, but I'd want to be around.”

**15.** “That reminds me, there's an old iron pipe down by the creek that I always assumed was a corner, but my brother-in-law says—”

**16.** “Oh, is that right. Well, alright then.”

**17.** “Well listen, I appreciate you taking the time. You've been very patient with an old man. Tell — who was it, Hank? — tell Hank to call whenever suits him.”

   > 👀 By here the call should be near the cap. The wind-down should fire and be warm, not abrupt.

**18.** “You too. Bye now.”

## Notes

The ONLY script that should hit the cap. Check three things: the wind-down fired before the hard stop, it named Hank and promised the information would reach him, and the facts gathered before it fired were all kept.
