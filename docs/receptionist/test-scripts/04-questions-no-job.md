# 04 · Just has questions, no job

**Who is calling** — Curtis Nakamura, thinking about buying land in a year, wants to understand the process.

**What this is testing** — There is no job here. The agent should be helpful, keep it short, and NOT run the full intake on somebody who has nothing to survey.

**Roughly** 3 minutes · **awkward**

---

## The call

**1.** “Hey there. I don't actually have a job for you — I'm just trying to learn. I'm looking at buying some land maybe next year and I keep seeing 'ALTA survey' and 'boundary survey' and I don't know the difference. Curtis Nakamura, by the way.”

**2.** “Sure.”

**3.** “So do I need a survey to buy land, or is that only if the bank wants it?”

   > 👀 It should not invent an answer. Taking the question down for Hank is correct.

**4.** “And how long is a survey good for? Like if it was done ten years ago is that still fine?”

**5.** “No, I really don't have a property yet. I'm looking at a couple of listings but nothing's under contract.”

   > 👀 It should stop asking for a property address once told there is not one.

**6.** “Curtis, C-U-R-T-I-S. Nakamura, N-A-K-A-M-U-R-A. 512-555-0119.”

**7.** “Yeah, that'd be great if he could call me. I'm not in a rush though.”

**8.** “No that's all. Thanks a lot.”

---

## It should end up with

- firstName Curtis
- lastName Nakamura
- phone
- his two questions recorded verbatim

## It should NEVER have asked about

- acreage
- structures
- documents
- propertyAddress

*This is the list that matters. An agent that gathers everything by asking about
everything has failed at the only thing separating it from a form.*

## Notes

A four-question interrogation of a man with no property is the failure. He should be off the phone in three minutes feeling well treated.
